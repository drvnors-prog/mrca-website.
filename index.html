Sound as a measured therapy

From listening
to prescribing.

We convert musical sound into defined therapeutic parameters, then design tailored sound prescriptions for specific neurological disorders, with a clear dose, duration and delivery.

Explore our research Collaborate with us

Most music therapy begins with how a person feels when they listen. We begin with what a specific sound does to the nervous system, and whether that effect can be measured, reproduced and prescribed.

Input · musical sound · many frequencies Decompose · measure · map Output · prescribed signal · e.g. 40 Hz gamma pulse

Our difference

Two patients with the same disorder should not receive music chosen by taste alone.

Music has healed for thousands of years, from the ragas of the Indian tradition to the hymns of ancient Greece. Modern science shows that sound entrains brain rhythms, shapes movement and influences sleep. We treat music as a physical signal that can be measured, decomposed and engineered.

Conventional approach

Music chosen by experience

Selection follows preference, mood and memory. Valuable for wellbeing, but hard to standardise across patients.

MRCA approach

Sound prescribed by evidence

Selection follows the condition and the patient's neural profile. Every parameter is specified so it can be tested and reproduced.

  • Starts withWhat the sound does to the brain
  • SelectionFrequency, pulse rate, tempo, modulation
  • DoseSession length, frequency, delivery
  • Measured byEEG, gait, sleep stages, clinical scores

Vision, aim, purpose

Sound, prescribed with the precision of medicine.

Vision

A world where sound is measured, dosed and matched to the brain it is meant to help.

Aim

To translate music into objective sound parameters and validate tailored sound prescriptions for neurological conditions.

Purpose

  1. Decode musical sound into frequency, rhythm, tempo, amplitude, modulation and timbre.
  2. Link each parameter to measurable outcomes in specific disorders.
  3. Design prescriptions with defined dose, duration and delivery.
  4. Unite neuroscience, acoustics, engineering, medicine, music and traditional knowledge.
  5. Share findings openly with clinicians, patients and researchers.

Research program

From signal to prescription, in six stages.

We begin where sound already shows a measurable neural effect, then build the prescription model outward.

  1. 01

    Sound decomposition

    Break music into carrier frequency, pulse rate, tempo, amplitude modulation, spectral shape, timbre and loudness.

  2. 02

    Neural response mapping

    Record how each parameter changes brain activity with EEG, MEG and fMRI, plus heart rate variability, gait and sleep.

  3. 03

    Patient profiling

    Identify each patient's neural signature: resting EEG rhythms, hearing profile and symptom pattern.

  4. 04

    Prescription design

    Match parameters to the profile with a defined dose, session length and delivery method.

  5. 05

    Clinical testing

    Pilot studies, then randomised controlled trials against active and sham sound controls.

  6. 06

    Translation

    Clinical guidelines, a prescription platform, and regulatory approval where required.

Our scientific stance. Popular claims about specific healing frequencies, such as 432 Hz or solfeggio tones, and about binaural beats currently have weak or inconsistent evidence. We treat them as hypotheses to test, never as foundations.

Traditional knowledge, including the Indian raga tradition, is a rich source of hypotheses. Studying it with modern measurement is part of our work.
